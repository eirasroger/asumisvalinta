Is it cheaper to rent or to buy? The answer depends on the area, on how long you plan to stay and on a few assumptions. This guide compares the same flat in two Helsinki areas with the Asumisvalinta comparison and shows where the differences come from.

## How the comparison works

The comparison looks at three ways to live in the same flat: renting, right of occupancy and buying. Each option starts with the same amount of money and spends the same amount each month. When one option is cheaper in a month, the difference is saved.

At the end of the period everything is turned into money: the flat is sold, the right-of-occupancy fee is refunded, the savings are withdrawn and taxes are paid. The option that leaves the most money is the cheapest under the chosen assumptions. The [methodology](/methodology) explains the calculation in full.

## Example: a 50 m² one-bedroom flat in two areas

Both examples are 50 m² one-bedroom flats, and every assumption is the comparison's default. The figures were calculated on 5 October 2026 with Statistics Finland's latest data: prices per m² from the first quarter of 2026 (preliminary) and rents per m² from the second quarter.

| | Kallio (00530) | Etelä-Vuosaari (00980) |
|---|---|---|
| Price per m² | €4,822 | €2,997 |
| Rent per m² a month | €21.65 | €16.40 |
| Price-to-rent ratio | 18.6 years | 15.2 years |
| Assumed price change a year | +0.7% | −1.2% |

**Money left at the end of the period:**

| | Buy | Rent | Right of occupancy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kallio, 5 years | €73,900 | €72,600 | €81,400 |
| Kallio, 15 years | €160,400 | €106,900 | €135,900 |
| Etelä-Vuosaari, 5 years | €32,200 | €46,100 | €53,000 |
| Etelä-Vuosaari, 15 years | €59,500 | €70,600 | €93,200 |

In Kallio, buying overtakes renting at five years, and over 15 years buying leaves the most money. In Etelä-Vuosaari, buying overtakes renting only at 21 years, and right of occupancy does best over both periods.

**Average monthly housing cost in the first year:**

| | Buy | Rent | Right of occupancy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kallio | €1,363 | €1,083 | €920 |
| Etelä-Vuosaari | €1,009 | €820 | €697 |

## Why the results differ

**Price development.** The comparison assumes prices develop like the area's price index did on average over the last ten years. In Kallio's price area the index rose by about 0.7% a year, in Etelä-Vuosaari's price area it fell by about 1.2% a year. The owner gains from a rise and loses from a fall; the tenant and the right-of-occupancy resident do not.

**Fixed costs per m².** The maintenance charge (€5.90 per m² a month in Greater Helsinki) and own repairs (€15.50 per m² a year) are the same in both areas. In a cheaper flat they are a bigger share of the total cost, which weakens buying in Etelä-Vuosaari.

**The price-to-rent ratio alone does not decide.** Kallio has the higher ratio, which would favour renting, yet buying does better there. Price development and fixed costs matter more. Read more in the guide on [reading the price-to-rent ratio](/oppaat/hinta-vuokrasuhde).

**Time.** Transfer tax and selling costs are paid once. The longer you stay, the smaller their share per year, so buying improves with time.

**Right of occupancy.** The comparison assumes a fee of 15% of the debt-free price and a monthly charge of 85% of the market rent. The low monthly cost and the index-linked refund make right of occupancy strong when prices rise slowly or fall. Read more in the guide [what is right of occupancy](/oppaat/asumisoikeus).

## Which assumptions change the result

The example uses the comparison's defaults: a fixed loan rate of 3.22%, own funds of 20% of the price, a 25-year loan, rents rising 2.1% a year and savings kept in a savings account. You can change every assumption in the comparison. These usually matter most:

- price development
- the loan rate
- the length of the period
- whether savings are invested or kept in an account.

The comparison also has a "What if" view that shows how the result changes if one assumption turns out differently.

## Limits

The figures are estimates based on area averages. The condition, year built, charges and renovations of a particular flat can change the result a lot. Right-of-occupancy flats are available only with a queue number, and not every area has free flats. The figures update as new data is published, so the comparison may differ from the figures in this guide.
