On the Asumisvalinta map you can colour the postal code areas by the price-to-rent ratio. The number answers a simple question: how many years of rent does the price of a flat equal?

## How it is calculated

The price-to-rent ratio is the price per m² divided by a year of rent per m²:

**price-to-rent ratio = price per m² ÷ (monthly rent per m² × 12)**

If the price is €4,800 per m² and the rent €20 per m² a month, a year of rent comes to €240 per m². The price then equals 20 years of rent.

On the map both figures are the latest from Statistics Finland: the price per m² of old flats in housing companies and the rent per m² of new free-market leases. The ratio is calculated separately for studios, one-bedroom flats and flats with two or more bedrooms.

## How to read it

- **A high ratio** means flats are expensive compared with rents. Renting is then relatively cheaper.
- **A low ratio** means flats are cheap compared with rents. Buying is then relatively cheaper.

Its inverse is the **gross rental yield**: a year of rent as a percentage of the price. A ratio of 20 equals a gross yield of 5%, a ratio of 25 a yield of 4%.

The ratio works best for comparing areas with each other. When two areas differ clearly, the difference shows where buying is more expensive relative to renting.

## What it leaves out

The price-to-rent ratio is a rough measure. It does not include:

- maintenance and finance charges that the owner pays
- loan interest, or what your own money could earn elsewhere
- transfer tax, selling costs and taxes
- renovations that the owner pays
- how prices and rents will develop
- differences between individual flats, such as condition, year built and location within the area.

The gross rental yield is therefore not an investor's net return, and a low ratio alone does not mean that buying is right for you.

## Hatched areas

When no figure has been published for a postal code area, the map uses the figure of a larger area: the price sub-area, the municipality, the region or the whole country. These areas are hatched on the map, and clicking one shows which level the figure comes from.
