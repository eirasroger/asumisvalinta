A first home in Finland is usually financed with a bank loan and your own savings. Many first-time buyers also use the state-supported ASP system, which adds better interest on savings, an interest subsidy and a state guarantee.

## Own funds and the loan cap

A housing loan can be at most **95%** of the fair value of the collateral. The same cap has applied to all buyers since 1 July 2026. The rest comes from your own funds or extra collateral.

## Transfer tax

First-time buyers pay transfer tax like other buyers: **1.5%** of the debt-free price of a flat in a housing company. On a debt-free price of €250,000, the tax is €3,750. The first-home exemption covered contracts signed before 1 January 2024.

## ASP saving

You can start ASP saving at 18 or over as a first-time buyer: someone who has bought less than half of any home before. Homes received as a gift or inheritance are left out of this. People aged 15 to 17 can also save, from their own earnings or funds they control.

- Save at least **10%** of the price of the home on an ASP account.
- Make at least **20 accepted monthly deposits** of €50 to €1,500 each.
- The savings earn **1%** tax-free interest a year. When you take the ASP loan, the bank adds extra interest of **2 to 4%**, agreed in the contract, on the savings used for the home.

## The ASP loan

The ASP loan can be up to nine times your accepted savings, and at most **90%** of the purchase price. The maximum loan is:

- **€230,000** for one person or **€345,000** for two in Espoo, Helsinki, Kauniainen, Oulu, Tampere, Turku and Vantaa;
- **€160,000** or **€240,000** elsewhere.

**Interest subsidy.** The state pays **70%** of the interest above **3.8%** of the remaining principal, for at most the first ten loan years. You can choose a bank's interest rate protection instead, and that choice is final.

**State guarantee.** A free state guarantee can be added to the loan. It covers up to 25% of the outstanding principal, at most €60,000, and is agreed in writing before the purchase.

**Term and use.** The loan term is at most 25 years when the loan is granted, and up to 29 years with repayment holidays. During the subsidy period the home is your permanent residence. You can rent it out or leave it empty for up to two years in total.
